About the hackathon

Why real-world health data

Hospitals and clinics generate enormous amounts of health data every day, yet most of it never reaches research: it is scattered across systems, inconsistent and too sensitive to share as it is.

The Israel–India Global Innovators Hackathon 2027 brings young talent from both countries together around one idea: Long Live Data. In the first track, teams make clinical records clean, safe and ready for AI and research. In the second, they use real-world data to explore what keeps people healthy for longer.

Teams work online with mentors from Israel and India and present their projects to an international jury in Coimbatore, alongside an Industry Day and an International Scientific Conference.

Two tracks

Choose the track that fits you

TRACK 01

FROM HEALTH RECORDS

For young AI, IT and data professionals

Work with real clinical records and turn them into data that research can actually use. Build the tools that make health data clean, safe and ready for AI and clinical research.

TRACK 02

TO RECORD HEALTH

For early-career biotech and life-science researchers

Use real-world health data to discover what keeps people healthy for longer. Explore longevity and anti-ageing science, and turn data into evidence for the next breakthroughs in health research.

Problem statements are kept general so that participants from different backgrounds can relate to them. Detailed challenges are developed by the organisers together with industry partners.

FAQ

Questions and answers

This page is updated as the programme develops.

Who can take part?
Final-year BSc students, graduates, young professionals and early-career researchers. Track 01 is aimed at AI, IT and data profiles; Track 02 at biotech and life-science profiles.
How big is a team?
Teams of 3–4 people. One team lead registers the whole team through the registration form.
Can I join one track only?
Yes. Each team chooses one track when registering.
Is the hackathon online or on site?
Both. Project work runs online from 17 to 31 January with mentoring by experts from Israel and India. Shortlisted teams defend their projects on site at SRCW, Coimbatore, on 1 February 2027.
How are teams selected for the hackathon?
After registration closes on 7 January, teams receive the challenges and submit a project proposal by 11 January. Ariel University and SRCW evaluate the proposals from 12 to 15 January and invite 20 teams per track.
What data will we work with?
Each track works with its own dataset prepared by the organisers. Details are shared with registered teams together with the challenges on 7 January.
What can we win?
Cash prizes in each track: INR 25,000 for the winner and INR 10,000 for the runner-up. Plus internship opportunities at TechGuard Labs and Promitei Data, the opportunity to join the Israel–India International Research Group on Longevity and Healthy Ageing, and merch and gifts from the hackathon sponsors.

About the day

Witness history

On 2 February two Israel–India institutions are born in Coimbatore: the Israel–Tamil Nadu Chamber of Commerce and the TechGuard Cyber Innovation Centre.

Industry Day is the business heart of Israel–India Innovation Week. Between the hackathon on 1 February and the scientific conference on 3 February, it brings together industry, government, academia and start-ups from Israel and Tamil Nadu at SREC SPARK in Coimbatore.

Two landmark moments define the day: the opening of the Israel–Tamil Nadu Chamber of Commerce and the foundation stone of the TechGuard Cyber Innovation Centre, the first AI, Data and Cyber Innovation Centre in Tamil Nadu. Together they turn Israel–India cooperation into lasting institutions: a platform for business, and a place for technology, talent and research.

Workshops, networking sessions and plenary discussions with leaders from Israel and India complete the programme.

About the conference

Where intelligent computing meets life science

A platform for scientists, researchers, educators, industry and students to explore the convergence of artificial intelligence, computing, mathematical sciences and life-science research.

The conference focuses on applying intelligent computing to challenges in bioinformatics, computational biology, healthcare, agriculture and environmental sustainability. It highlights how machine learning, data analytics, optimisation and mathematical frameworks accelerate discovery, improve decision-making and enable resource-efficient solutions.

Aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, it promotes research that benefits society, protects the environment and addresses pressing global challenges.
